Giving
Without Knowing

How major donors navigate political giving they cannot measure, and what that means for organizations raising resources.

Nearly $15 billion moved through the 2024 elections. Ask the donors giving the money what they most want to know before they give, and most name something nobody can tell them: whether the money will matter, how it will actually be spent, whether the candidate will follow through.

The critical finding

Major donors have rationally adapted to a question that cannot be answered.

60% of donors, and 8 of 11 advisors, named information that cannot realistically be known in advance. They do not respond by researching harder. They respond by building systems that make the unanswerable question unnecessary. That means deciding before the ask arrives, or handing the judgment to someone they trust.

92% decide in advance, through standing rules that settle most asks before any deliberation
56% hand the evaluation to a person or institution they trust
84% named a gap between their stated rules and their actual giving

The rules bend in one direction

  • Toward people. 64% describe relationship-driven exceptions, and most name the pattern themselves before being asked.
  • Not toward winning. 80% ask “does my gift matter?” rather than “can this candidate win?”, steering money to where dollars are scarce.
  • Loyalty attaches to people, not organizations. Every exit donors described followed a failure inside the organization: leadership, conduct, drift. None followed a lost election.

Method

36 semi-structured interviews with 25 high-net-worth donors and 11 professional donor advisors, a landscape review, and two decades of FEC and NCSBE campaign-finance data. All interviews were anonymized. Completed in fulfillment of the Executive Master of Public Affairs at Duke University’s Sanford School of Public Policy. Faculty advisor: Caryl Stern, PhD.
